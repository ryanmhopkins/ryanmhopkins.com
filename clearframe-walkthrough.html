Try ClearFrame without a personal photo.

A photo can carry more than the picture. Here’s a way to see what that means before using one of your own.

I’m the maker of ClearFrame, a photo metadata scanner and cleaner that runs in your browser. This is a walkthrough of my own product.

Camera and editing software can save information inside a photo file: dates, author names, camera details, sometimes GPS coordinates. What’s present depends on the file. ClearFrame lets you review supported metadata and choose blocks or fields to remove from a separate copy.

Start with the fictional sample

  1. Open ClearFrame and choose Try a sample image, just below the photo picker.
  2. The sample is a synthetic illustration with fictional author, description, and software information. It contains no personal photo or real location.
  3. Review the EXIF removal choice. In JPEG files, this removes the whole EXIF block, rather than just one tag inside it. Color and display information is kept by default.
  4. Choose Download clean copy. To inspect the result, choose Add photos and select that downloaded copy. The EXIF block removed from the sample should no longer appear.

Sample downloads count toward the same free allowance as other photos. There’s no account or payment needed to try this.

Then try an exported copy of your own

Keep your original photo in your archive. Add a copy you intend to share, inspect the detected metadata, and remove only what you want removed. After downloading, open it in your regular photo viewer and re-scan it before sharing.

Cleaning doesn’t resize or recompress the image. Removing orientation or color information can still change how a viewer displays it. Metadata cleaning also doesn’t remove anything visible in the picture, such as a street sign, face, or watermark, and it isn’t a guarantee of anonymity.

What stays on your device?

Photo bytes stay in your browser during scanning and cleaning. The website still contacts its hosting service to load the app, and optional anonymous website analytics are separate from photo processing. The privacy explanation covers the details and limits.

Free includes JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, HEIC, HEIF, AVIF, and TIFF, with 10 photo downloads per local day. Each photo inside a ZIP counts as a download.
